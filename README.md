Gorgonopsian Dental Replacement

Project Overview

This project investigates dental replacement patterns in Gorgonopsia, an extinct group of therapsids, using X-ray micro-computed tomography (µCT), 3D reconstructions and morphological measurements.

The research examines how teeth were replaced, differences in canine replacement arrangements, and how dental replacement patterns varied between specimens at different growth stages.

The project is based on my 2024 Honours research at the University of the Witwatersrand.

Research Objectives

* Investigate dental replacement patterns in six gorgonopsian specimens.
* Examine the arrangement of functional and replacement canine teeth.
* Compare dental characteristics across specimens.
* Explore differences in tooth replacement activity between growth stages.
* Consider the implications of these patterns for understanding ancestral theriodont dental replacement.

Specimens

Methodology

1. CT Imaging

X-ray micro-computed tomography was used to examine the internal dental structures of the fossil specimens.

2. Segmentation and 3D Reconstruction

Specimens were segmented and reconstructed in three dimensions to examine tooth positions, replacement teeth and dental structures.

3. Morphological Measurements

Five cranial measurements were recorded:

* Maximum cranial length
* Maximum cranial width
* Maxillary bicanine breadth
* Dental series length
* Antorbital snout length

The number of postcanine teeth was also recorded.

4. Comparative Analysis

Dental replacement arrangements were compared across specimens to investigate variation in canine replacement and differences associated with growth stages.

Key Findings

The research identified three upper canine replacement arrangements, differing in the number and position of functional and replacement canines.

The findings also indicated differences in replacement activity between growth stages, with juveniles showing more replacement activity. Double canine arrangements were more pronounced in the more derived taxa examined, including Aloposaurus and Aelurosaurus.

Alternating tooth replacement was also observed.
